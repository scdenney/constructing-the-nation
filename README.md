# Constructing the Nation: Identity and Historical Narratives in South Korean History Textbooks

**Steven Denney** (Leiden University) · **Aron van de Pol** (Leiden University)

**Last updated:** 19 June 2026

---

Schools are where states teach the nation, yet the institutions shaping that instruction vary. Regime type and the arrangements governing textbook authorship may both be reflected in national narratives, but they need not change together. This article separates the two through the first full-corpus computational analysis of South Korea's national history (*kuksa*) textbooks. We analyze all 67 volumes published between 1895 and 2016. Using large language models, we identify identity terms inductively from the corpus and track their meanings with sentence-level embeddings, capturing changes in both vocabulary and context. We find that regime type shows in the character of narrative change, while textbook production institutions set its timing and scale. Under authoritarian state monopoly, inherited identity terms were reorganized around ethnic belonging and colonial grievance. Democratic-era textbooks expanded the same vocabulary into contexts of reconciliation and sovereignty disputes, but this expansion remained overwhelmingly ethnic. In textbooks published after 2002, one quarter of sentences mentioning the *nation* (*minjok*) contain explicitly ethnic terms, while fewer than one percent contain civic or multiethnic language. The timing of change follows institutional reform rather than regime transition. The authoritarian reorganization accumulated across the first two curriculum cycles, and no detectable shift accompanies democratization in 1987. A first wave of change emerged under the Sixth Curriculum (1992–97), while the state still retained authorship, and a broader second wave followed the reopening of modern-history authorship in 2002. Regime change reaches the national narrative through the institutions that produce it.

**Keywords**: national identity, history textbooks, Korean history, natural language processing
